# Stratum — first verified run inside Rhino

**2026-09-13 · Rhino 8.36.26251.14001 · Windows · document units: millimetres**

The first time this plug-in has been exercised in a live Rhino and the results
written down. Everything below was measured, not inferred. Where a number is
quoted it came out of the running document.

Deliberately run in a **millimetre** document rather than the inch/foot path
`START-HERE.md` describes, because millimetres are the real working unit here
and the 211 offline assertions cannot cover unit conversion — they run without a
document, so `inchToModel` is never anything but 1.

## 6 · THE FAILURE — junctions do not resolve

Three walls drawn: two W1 meeting at a right-angle **corner**, and one W2
partition running into the middle of the first as a **tee**.

Every layer solid of each wall was tested against every layer solid of every
other wall: bounding boxes first, then a real `Brep.CreateBooleanIntersection`
and a volume on anything that survived.

```
bbox-overlapping pairs tested : 54
real volumetric clashes       : 54
total clashing volume         : 32,015,594 mm3 = 0.0320 m3
worst single pair             : 4,470,959 mm3
                                W2 2x4 stud  <->  W1 2x6 stud
```

**54 of 54.** Nothing is cut back. At the corner both walls run straight past
each other and every layer of one occupies the same space as every layer of the
other — there is no mitre. At the tee the W2 gypsum and studs drive straight
through the W1 gypsum, studs, OSB, polyiso, WRB, furring and siding — there is
no notch and nothing stops at a face.

**`BimRebuild` does not fix it.** Ran it — "3 of 3 elements rebuilt" — and the
clash count was identical: 54 of 54, same 0.0320 m3.

### Why this is not a surprise, and why it still matters

`README.md` claims, in the present tense:

> where two walls meet at a corner, both run past it and are cut back on the
> angle bisector so every layer mitres cleanly ... the tee ties to structure ...
> the through wall is notched over the width of the arriving core so the two
> never occupy the same space.

That is not what happens. But the same README already names the reason, in its
own words:

> the tee arithmetic — that the arriving core lands exactly on the through
> wall's core face, that its finish layers stop short at the face, and that the
> notch is exactly as wide as the arriving core ... **the logic is tested, the
> boolean result isn't.**

Exactly so. `WallJoiner` and `WallJunctions` compute the right planes — 211
assertions say so — and nothing applies them to the solids. The arithmetic is
correct and unused.

### What it blocks

Everything downstream of a junction, which is most of the value:

- **Quantities double-count.** 0.032 m3 of material exists twice in a
  three-wall model. A takeoff from this is wrong, and wrong in the direction
  that costs money.
- **Sections are unreadable at exactly the place a section is taken.** A corner
  or a tee is where a detail gets cut. Overlapping poché there is not a drawing.
- **The "model and the details agree" claim fails** at the junction, which is
  the claim the plug-in is for.

### The fix is probably one field, not a rewrite

Revit's `CompoundStructureLayer` carries a **`Priority`**, and priority is what
decides which layer wins where two walls meet. `AssemblyLayer` has 18 fields —
`Function`, `Side`, `IsCore`, three `EdgeResolution`s for jamb/head/sill — and
**no priority**.

The same rule is already written down, independently, in House Anatomy's
`GAPS.md` Gap AT:

> materials run into their same material in a T; the studs go thru till they
> touch studs, the gyp till it meets the first instance of gyp

That is layer priority. One integer on `AssemblyLayer`, consumed by
`WallJunctions`, would resolve the tee, the corner, the three- and four-way
junctions the README says are "left square", and Gap AT — one mechanism instead
of four.

# Addendum — second pass, same day

Colour-coded plan cuts through both junctions (screenshots in `_shots/`).
Two defects found. Both produce wrong construction documents, which is the
only reason they matter.

## D-A · Corners are always mitred. There is no butt join.

`WallJoiner.MakeMiter` sets `FacePlane = CorePlane` — "a mitre cuts every layer
on the same plane" (its own comment). `JointKind` offers `None`, `Miter`, `Tee`.
There is no butt option and no way to ask for one.

Consequence: a 45° line runs from the inside corner to the outside corner
through every layer of the assembly. It is real geometry, not a display
artefact, so it will be drawn in any plan or section taken from the model, and
a builder reads it as a joint that does not exist. Nobody mitres a stud corner.

Revit does not do this. Revit resolves a corner by **layer priority**: the
higher-priority layer runs through, the lower-priority one butts into its face.
At a normal 90° corner one wall's studs run through, the other wall's studs butt
into them, the exterior cladding wraps the outside continuously, and each wall's
gypsum butts into the other's. No diagonal anywhere. Revit mitres only when you
explicitly ask, or at odd angles where a butt would look worse.

This is the same integer already identified in §6 above, and the same rule
written independently in House Anatomy's `GAPS.md` Gap AT. It resolves the
corner, the tee, the three- and four-way junctions, and Gap AT.

What it takes:

1. `int Priority` on `AssemblyLayer` (Revit convention: 1 structure,
   2 substrate, 3 thermal/air, 4 finish 1, 5 finish 2). Core defaults to 1.
2. `WallJoint.PlaneFor(bool isCore)` becomes `PlaneFor(int layerIndex)` — a
   cut plane per layer instead of two. The tee already needed two planes for
   the same reason; this is that generalisation finished.
3. `Miter()` keeps a per-layer resolution: for each layer of wall A, find the
   layer of B it runs into; lower priority number runs through to the far face
   of its counterpart, higher number stops at the winner's near face. Equal
   priority — one wall wins by join order, and that order must be flippable,
   as it is in Revit.
4. Keep the bisector mitre as an explicit `JointKind.Miter`, chosen, not
   assumed.

**Then the drawing side, which priority alone does not fix.** Two butted layers
of the same material are still two separate solids, so a section will draw a
line between them. Revit suppresses that edge when the materials match. Stratum
will need the same rule wherever the 2D output is generated, or the confusing
line comes back one step later in a different place.

## D-B · A notch through a wall discards everything past it.

`WallBuilder.cs` ~line 157:

```csharp
var notched = Brep.CreateBooleanDifference(new[] { brep }, new[] { cutter }, tol);
if (notched != null && notched.Length > 0)
  brep = notched.Length == 1 ? notched[0]
       : Brep.JoinBreps(notched, tol)?.FirstOrDefault() ?? notched[0];
```

A tee landing mid-wall notches the through wall. The cutter is a bounded slice,
so the difference correctly returns **two** disjoint solids. `JoinBreps` cannot
join two disjoint solids, so it returns both — and `.FirstOrDefault()` keeps one
and silently throws the other away.

Measured in the live document. Test run W2, 6000 mm long, tee landing at
2930 mm:

| layer | x extent | length | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 03 Gypsum 5/8" (far side) | −11220 → −5159 | 6060 | intact |
| 02 Wood stud 2x4 | −11220 → −5175 | 6044 | intact |
| 01 Gypsum 5/8" (notched side) | −11220 → **−8289** | **2930** | **truncated** |

That layer is a 6-face box. Not a notched solid — a shortened one. The wall has
no gypsum on that face for the last 3.07 m, and the plan shows it (image
`32-overall-plan.png`: the light band along the top of the run stops dead at the
tee).

Fix: keep every piece. A layer must become a list of solids, not one solid, from
the notch onward. `JoinBreps` is the wrong call here — the pieces are meant to
be disjoint.

## Correction to §6's verdict

The earlier run reported the tee as resolving. At the junction itself it does:
the stem's core passes through the through wall's finish and lands on its core;
the stem's cladding stops at the face. That part is right. What is wrong is what
happens to the through wall beyond the junction — D-B above. "The tee resolves"
was true of the 300 mm I looked at and false of the 3 m I did not.
